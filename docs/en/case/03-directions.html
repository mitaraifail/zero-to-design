Pulse · Phase 3 Design Directions

Based on the product definition and reference collection, we generated 4 distinct design-language directions. Each direction includes color, typography, layout, signature motion, and a mini interface preview. Pick one, or combine elements from multiple directions.

Product Analysis

One-liner
Roll revenue, user, and event data into a clear homepage—know in 30 seconds every morning whether anything is off.
Target Users
Indie developers / one-person companies aged 25–40, currently checking numbers manually across Stripe, GA, SQL, and Excel.
Core Mood
Calm + Speed: reduce anxiety, glance and go.
Key Differentiator
Proactively surface the "today status" every morning instead of waiting for users to dig for it.
Conversion Goal
Default state: scan in 30 seconds and leave. Anomaly state: spot it at a glance and drill down in one click.
Aesthetic Constraints
Light mode only; no purple, no blue as primary, no gradients.

AMorning Paper

Open Pulse like a morning paper: warm, clear, today’s status read in one glance.

Direction Rationale i Why this direction fits Pulse: building on Phase 1’s Calm + Speed and your Phase 2 preference for Monocle’s "order within calm," it uses a warm neutral base + green status color to turn anxious mornings into a predictable 30-second ritual.

Takes Monocle’s editorial order but replaces cool gray with a softer warm-white base; a subtle "normal heartbeat" motion echoes Calm, making the default state feel steady and unobtrusive.

Typography System i Display is for large headings, Body for running text, Mono for numbers, code, and labels. Each typeface is annotated with weight, size, and line height.

Display "Bricolage Grotesque" 700 40px/1.15 For page headlines; slightly humanistic, never cold.

Layout Pattern i How key areas are organized: Hero is the above-the-fold visual area, CTA is the call-to-action area, listing area is the content-list area.

Top nav + full-width "Today" summary card (taking up 50% of the first screen) with 3 key metrics; below, a two-column split: recent event stream on the left, quick actions on the right. Strategy: put the "glance" at the top and the "tap again" below.

Signature Motion i The most memorable micro-animation in this direction. Hover over the summary card in the mini preview, or watch the gentle glow rhythm after the page loads.

Calm Pulse: When all metrics are normal, the "Today" summary card emits an extremely soft green ambient pulse, like a steady heartbeat. It is not an alert; it tells the user "the system is watching for you." Once an anomaly appears, the pulse stops and the relevant metric gets a soft solid highlight to guide the drill-down.

BSignal & Noise

Automatically dims normal numbers and pushes only what "needs your eyes" to the front.

Direction Rationale i Why this direction fits Pulse: it directly echoes Phase 1’s core differentiator—"proactively tell you today’s status"—using visual weight to express priority.

Takes "scan and go" to the extreme: normal metrics fade in first and stay low-contrast; metrics needing attention arrive last and get emphasized. The coral accent only appears when something is off, staying nearly invisible on normal days.

Body "Manrope" 500 15px/1.5 Medium weight makes data cards feel fuller.
Mono "JetBrains Mono" 600 16px/1.3 Amounts and percentages are bolder and easier to scan.

Layout Pattern i How key areas are organized.

Asymmetric two-column: the left 55% is a giant "Today" panel with 3 oversized metrics; the right 45% is the event stream. Normal metrics shrink their presence; anomalies are amplified. Strategy: use visual weight to directly express "what deserves your attention."

Signature Motion i The most memorable micro-animation in this direction. Watch how the numbers come into focus one by one as the page loads.

Focus Reveal: On page load, all metrics appear low-contrast and slightly blurred; normal metrics quickly settle into a readable but unobtrusive state; metrics needing attention come into focus last, accompanied by a left-to-right drawn underline. It turns "proactive alerting" into visual storytelling instead of a popup interruption.

CMonocle Desk

Editorial desk: using rules, whitespace, and density to arrange data into a calm, readable morning paper.

Direction Rationale i Why this direction fits Pulse: closest to your favorite Monocle reference from Phase 2—editorial feel + balanced information density.

Translates the magazine language of "masthead + columns + hairline rules" into a data dashboard. Serif headings bring a humanistic editorial tone; forest-green accent replaces cold blue, making the whole feel cultured without being luxurious.

Layout Pattern i How key areas are organized.

Signature Motion i The most memorable micro-animation in this direction. Watch how hairline rules draw the grid themselves as the page loads.

Rules Draw: After page load, horizontal and vertical hairline rules extend left-to-right and top-to-bottom as if drawn by a ruler. It turns "order" from static to dynamic, implying "data is being organized into a clean daily report."

DSoft Utility

Friendly, tool-like, tactile: like a tidy desk where cards guard the gates for you.

Direction Rationale i Why this direction fits Pulse: a counterpoint to Monocle’s editorial feel, testing whether you prefer a more approachable, tool-like dashboard.

Counter direction: no hairline rules or serif; instead, soft cards, rounded corners, and hover feedback. It feels closer to the intuition of common SaaS dashboards, but warm green and restrained shadows avoid "enterprise coldness."

Body "Manrope" 400 15px/1.55 Body shares the same family as Display, reducing visual noise.
Mono "JetBrains Mono" 500 14px/1.4 Amounts, percentages, timestamps.

Layout Pattern i How key areas are organized.

Card-based dashboard: top nav, welcome card, 2×2 metric-card grid, quick-action chips, event-card list. Strategy: use cards to naturally group information so the user’s finger/eye processes one chunk at a time.

Signature Motion i The most memorable micro-animation in this direction. Hover over a card, or click a quick-action chip.

Tactile Cards: All interactive cards lift slightly by 3px on hover and spread a warm, soft shadow; they sink 1px when pressed. It gives the data dashboard a tangible, physical feel, reducing the cold distance of "looking at numbers."

How to Choose

These four directions are rapid experience drafts for locking in color, typography, layout, and motion. Once selected, Phase 4 will produce a more complete and realistic homepage design.

  • Already happy with one direction: Reply with the letter A / B / C / D; you can also mix, e.g., A’s color system + C’s masthead layout + D’s hover motion.
  • Not satisfied yet: Reply None of these, try something more ×× (e.g., warmer / bolder / more minimal); or A’s colors are nice, but the type feels too cold—swap to a more humanistic font and regenerate.
